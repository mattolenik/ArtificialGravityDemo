# Terrain edits: follow-ups

What the edits plan (`2026-10-27-terrain-edits-C.md`, branch `terrain-edits`) left open on purpose, so the next slice's brief can pick it up. Nothing here blocks digging, walking in a trench or saving the pit; the items the plan closed are marked in `2026-10-27-terrain-slice-1-followups.md`.

## Carry into the next slice's brief

- **Edits in the far window** (decision 12). The 8 m ring and the 32 m whole-station mesh regenerate from the macro maps and show no edits; a 1 m pit is below their resolution and a hand tool works within 256 m of the viewer, so the only effect is an edit appearing when its tile crosses from the ring into the 1 m window. A bigger tool (a blast, a collapse) needs a coarse edit layer, e.g. a downsampling apply of the main-window blobs into far tiles (up to 64 blobs per far tile, many not in RAM). `far_terrain.gd`'s header says so.
- **A second collider patch per rigid body** (decision 17's design; the slice 1 item stays open). This plan only holds a body the live patch does not cover (the `terrain_ground` group, disabled and re-enabled by `Terrain._process`), so boxes near a frozen player no longer fall to the hull but also do not collide with anything while held. `ColliderPatch` is parametric (any sample count and centre): one `ColliderPatch(65)` per body or cluster outside the big patch, rebaked when the body moves more than 16 m from its centre, freed when the big patch covers it again; about a 17 KB readback and a 0.2 ms Jolt build each. A held box that leaves the group stays disabled.
- **R2 (noise octaves) was not touched here**; the noise-octaves plan closed it. Edits are absolute per-cell overrides under an `edit_mask`, so retuning `detail.glsl` later changes the unedited cells only: a stamped cell keeps the values it was stamped with.
- **Jolt's per-block 8-bit height quantisation** as a suspect for the 18 cm collider mismatch at altitude (15 cm p99 after the octaves plan, 64 cm max on bare rock). If Godot's Jolt heightfield stores each block's heights as 8-bit offsets between the block's lowest and highest sample, a block spanning a steep face of H m quantises at H / 255 m. Unmeasured: compare the shape's `map_data` with what Jolt returns for the same cells (a ray cast grid) on a cliff row before blaming the bake.
- **Window memory** is 23 bytes per cell (6.0 MB at 512, 24 MB at 1024) because the slice 1 builder chose `R32_SFLOAT` over the spec's `R16`. The edit layer adds `edit_mask` (256 KB at 512, 1 MB at 1024, unused on the far window), `_up` 393 KB and `_down` 1.57 MB. Re-check against the spec's budget when the window grows to 1024.
- **Blobs are uncompressed in RAM**: 393 KB per edited tile (128 × 128 × 24 B), so a thousand edited tiles are 400 MB. They are compressed only on disk. Compress in RAM, or keep the blobs of tiles far from the viewer on disk only, when a profile shows it.
- **A stamped tile evicted and re-entered within the 2-frame readback applies its old blob** (decision 4's accepted limit), and nothing re-applies when the pack lands. `TerrainWindow.regenerate()` within the same two frames does the same. The dig slice's op kernel should carry a per-slot generation.
- **The stamp radius is in cells, not metres.** A cell is 1 m along the axis and (R − h) dθ across: 1.23 m on the hull and 0.64 m at h_sea + h_mtn with the default `WorldParams`, so a 5-cell disc is an ellipse in metres (5 by 6 m on the hull, 5 by 3 m on the highest rock). A tool sized in metres needs a radius per axis.
- **GPU timestamp outliers and the budgets they miss** (`docs/terrain-research.md` section 6.8, "Edit slice measurements"). Every `edit_run.tscn` run on Vulkan had three to six `edit` or `bake_edit` lists over their budgets of 0.5 and 1 ms (the worst 2.3 to 4.7 ms) against medians of 0.06 to 0.12 ms, in the standing phases or while walking; the slice 1 bake max and the old bake shader had the same spikes. Whether they are the clock behind `capture_timestamp` on MoltenVK or real stalls is not known: capture a few with a GPU frame capture, or look for a frame that also carries a pipeline compile or a texture upload. Also over a stated expectation, within budget: the big bake's median is 0.20 to 0.26 ms against the 0.2 ms expected, and the GPU frame p99 reached 6.3 to 6.7 ms in some runs' dig or trench (means 2.9 to 4.1 ms).

## Deferred by the task reviews

- **The pack pass stores every cell's values**, so a tile with a few edits saves as about 188 KB (a 20-stamp pit measured) rather than the kilobytes decision 1 expected. Write zeros for the unmasked cells in `edit.glsl` mode 1 and zstd collapses them; the apply pass reads only masked cells.
- **A failed edit-bake readback leaves `_pending_edit` set** and, for a first zone, a hole in the big patch with no shape under it while `covers` stays true. On that failure branch, request a big rebake without the hole.
- **The save-at-exit path has not been exercised by a run**: dig, quit within 30 s (before the autosave), restart. `Terrain._exit_tree` writes the file; the restart check's autosave fired first. A stamp whose readback has not landed at quit is lost, by design.
- **Digging in place for 2 s makes 60° pit walls the player cannot climb** (20 stamps of 0.25 m at 10 a second in a 5-cell disc). `edit_run.gd`'s trench digs on the move for that reason. A tool needs a slower rate, a wider disc or a limit on the slope it leaves, or the player needs a way out.
- **Rim cells at exactly the stamp radius are masked with zero lift**: a face cell on the rim becomes dirt. Tighten the stamp's `dist > radius` skip in `edit.glsl` to `>=` if that shows.
- **Cells with `cliff == 255` at the height cap have no jump** (generator quirk): the first such cell found by `debug_window.gd --cliff` is clipped by the cap, not a rim.
- **Two sources for the hole cell in `ColliderPatch`**: `_bake` reads `zone`, `rebuild` binds it as an argument to the landing. Pass the hole cell into `_bake` instead of a bool, so there is one.

## Measured limits (M1 Max, Godot 4.7.2, 1152 × 648, three Vulkan runs)

- Edit (stamp plus pack) 0.08 to 0.12 ms median per stamp; apply 0.025 to 0.035 ms per tile; `bake_edit` 0.05 to 0.07 ms; `bake` 0.20 to 0.26 ms median, 0.8 ms max; detail 0.09 to 0.14 ms per tile (all budgets in section 6.8 hold at the median).
- `map_data` CPU 0.22 ms per edit bake and 2.7 ms per big bake; frame interval 8.33 ms with none over 16 ms in the dig, the raise or the trench, on both drivers.
- Metal still returns zero GPU timestamps: the GPU figures are MoltenVK numbers, the Metal run checks CPU times and behaviour.
